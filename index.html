Vektor

An autonomous, cart-scale mobile robot for indoor mapping and navigation using visual SLAM, multi-camera depth, and the ROS 2 Nav2 stack—implemented on NVIDIA Jetson Orin with an Arduino Mega bridge for low-level drive, sensing, and manual override.

Abstract

Vektor targets reliable autonomous operation in typical indoor workspaces—corridors, labs, and open office areas—where a wheeled cart must construct a usable map online and thereafter plan and execute collision-free motion to goals. The system couples Luxonis OAK-D stereo RGB-D sensing in four orientations, converting depth cues into rangefinder-style observations suitable for occupancy mapping. On the computation side, ROS 2 Humble on Ubuntu 22.04 integrates RTAB-Map visual SLAM and Nav2 for global and local navigation, layered costmaps, and recovery behaviors while wheel odometry from encoders—and optional fusion—anchors short horizon motion between visual updates.

An Arduino Mega handles time-critical interfaces: PWM actuation for the differential drivetrain, quadrature decoding, ultrasonic proximity on the leading corners for guard-rail prompting, joystick input for supervised teleop, and a serial bridge to ROS nodes on the Jetson. The result is a complete autonomy stack from sensors to actuator commands that can map a new environment during an exploration phase and transition to repeatable goal-to-goal missions with RViz-assisted goal setting and monitoring.

Introduction and objectives

Indoor service and logistics prototypes increasingly rely on mobile bases that localize in previously unknown or partially modeled buildings. Practical systems must unify perception, estimation, motion planning, and safe actuation—and stay debuggable across field deployment. This work presents Vektor, a full-stack robot built around commodity depth cameras and an open robotics middleware, with emphasis on modular bring-up paths for mapping versus navigation missions.

The principal goals pursued in Vektor are:

  • Maintain accurate environmental models using RGB-D aided SLAM augmented by synthesized range observations from peripheral cameras—improving completeness around the cart silhouette.
  • Field a production-grade navigation stack (Nav2) with standard costmaps, planners, and controllers suitable for differential platforms.
  • Preserve human-in-the-loop operability through joystick teleoperation, serially mediated safety inputs, and clear separation between low-level motor control and high-level autonomy.

System architecture

Hardware

Perception and autonomy execute on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin, while actuation and raw encoder streams terminate on an Arduino Mega. Four Luxonis OAK-D RGB-D sensors provide a forward primary stream for mapping and ancillary side/rear coverage owned by downstream nodes that reduce depth to planar scans. Ultrasonic rangefinders on the front quarters supply inexpensive near-field alerting complementary to the depth-derived obstacle signal. Mechanical packaging follows a rectangular cart (~86 × 43 × 89 cm) with differential drive centrally located wheel pairs and caster support at the longitudinal centerline front and rear, yielding predictable turning poles for planar navigation models.

Software

The software stack aligns with ROS 2 community practice: parameterized launch graphs switch between slam test and navigation configurations; RTAB-Map ingests calibrated camera topics and synthesized laser scans; Nav2 subscribes to the map and odometry frames published by state estimation. DepthAI SDK v3 interfaces control camera exposure and synchronizes depth with color where required. Visualization and operator supervision use RViz2; bagging and offline replay support regression of perception and control changes without repeated hardware runs.

Mapping, localization, and autonomous navigation

Mapping mode launches the robot state publisher, base controller, camera publishers, depth scan synthesis, and RTAB-Map. As the platform is driven—manually or under scripted exploration—RGB-D keyframes and scan constraints populate a consistent occupancy representation suitable for downstream planning exports.

Navigation mode reuses the fused odometry backbone and swaps in Nav2 planners, controller servers, and behavior recovery plugins. Static and inflation layers ingest the SLAM-maintained occupancy grid; local footprints reflect the cart geometry tuned in parameter files controlling footprint padding and obstacle inflation radii proportional to perceptual latency.

Transitions across modes prioritize repeatable topic naming and calibrated transforms so localization continuity is preserved wherever possible; failure recovery leverages Nav2-assisted behaviors (e.g., rotate in place, backup) guarded by ultrasonic and cliff logic implemented at the microcontroller edge when enabled.
