The Problem: Transport & Docking

  • Massive Workload: Patient tables weigh 670 lbs (300 kg) unloaded. This contributes to >50% of technologists reporting occupational injuries and a record 16.2% MR tech vacancy rate.
  • Collision Damage: While modern e-drives assist with pushing, they still require manual steering. This often results in crashing into door frames and walls, causing equipment stress and patient discomfort.
  • The Docking Bottleneck: A single technologist pushing from the rear has severe blind spots, making alignment with the scanner bore a slow, tedious process of micro-adjustments.
  • The Goal: Build an autonomous foundation for corridor navigation and precise auto-docking, removing this physical burden.

Why It Matters (Clinical & Business Impact)

  • Operational Efficiency: As MRI scan times decrease, non-scan tasks (like manually pushing the 670 lb table) become the primary bottleneck for throughput.
  • Greater Patient Focus (“Eyes Up”): Currently, technologists stare at the floor and wheels to avoid collisions. Autonomy allows them to keep their “eyes up” and focused entirely on patient care and comfort.
  • Talent Retention: With a 16.2% vacancy rate, hospitals cannot afford to burn out staff on heavy labor. Automating transport is crucial for workplace satisfaction and reducing the >50% injury rate.
  • Competitive Edge: Competitors (like Siemens) are already patenting patient table autonomous navigation. Proving this technology keeps us ahead of the curve.

Why Build a Custom MR-Specific Robot? (The “Build vs. Buy” Decision)

  • The MR Constraint: High-field MRI suites are hazardous environments for standard robotics. Strong magnetic fields restrict the use of typical sensors like spinning LiDAR (due to ferrous motors/magnets).
  • The “Build vs Buy” Reality: There are no off-the-shelf consumer or warehouse robots designed for this unique constraint and the specific geometry of patient table docking.
  • The Vektor Approach: By building our own camera-centric (LiDAR-free) prototype, we maintain complete control over the safety architecture, sensor choices, and software stack required for eventual clinical integration.

The Hardware Foundation (Ground-Up Integration)

Compute
NVIDIA Jetson Orin running Ubuntu 22.04 + ROS 2 Humble
Perception
4× Luxonis OAK-D cameras (stereo depth + RGB + IMU on-device)
Drive Layer
Arduino Mega + Cytron MDDS30 motor driver + wheel encoders
Safety
HC-SR04 ultrasonic array for near-field obstacle detection
Power
24V traction battery with regulated DC-DC rails
Specs
86cm × 43cm × 89cm chassis, differential drive, 12.7cm wheels

System Architecture (The Data Pipeline)

  • Perception → Map: OAK-D cameras feed RTAB-Map for RGB-D SLAM
  • Map → Plan: Nav2 stack reads the map and plans safe paths
  • Plan → Motion: Velocity commands flow through Arduino to motors
  • Safety Layer: Ultrasonics feed a safety monitor that scales velocity near obstacles

Perception & Mapping (RGB-D SLAM)

  • VPU Offloading: Depth computed on-camera, preserving Jetson CPU
  • Scan Merger: Custom node combines 4 depth cameras into unified 360° scan
  • RTAB-Map: Visual SLAM using RGB + Depth for loop closure in repetitive corridors
  • Output: 2D occupancy grid for Nav2 costmaps

Navigation Stack

  • Path Planning: NavfnPlanner uses A* algorithm on the global costmap
  • Path Following: RegulatedPurePursuit controller tracks the planned path
  • Costmap Layers: Static (saved map) + Obstacle (live scans) + Inflation (safety buffer)
  • Safety Monitor: Ultrasonics scale velocity when obstacles are within 50cm

Auto-Docking System

  • Visual Docking: AprilTag (tag36h11) detected by front OAK-D camera using pupil-apriltags library
  • Voice Control: Offline speech recognition (Vosk) + text-to-speech (Piper) via Jabra mic/speaker
  • Approach Algorithm: Speed scales from 0.10 m/s (far) → 0.02 m/s (close), stops when ultrasonic < 10cm AND tag centered within 3cm
  • Commands: “Hello” wakes robot → “Dock” or “Go home” triggers docking → “Undock” backs away

Results in Action

  • Perception Verified: Depth cameras correctly project obstacles into local occupancy grid
  • SLAM Working: RTAB-Map builds persistent 2D map of cluttered environments
  • Full Autonomy: Nav2 plans and executes collision-free paths
  • Auto-Docking: Voice integrated, sub-cm precision alignment achieved
