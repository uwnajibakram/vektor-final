Camera-Based Autonomous Navigation for MR Patient Tables

Phantom differential-drive prototype Vektor explores whether perimeter RGB-D perception with on-device inference can supersede standalone lidar while supplying semantic scene context for clinically relevant indoor navigation—all integrated through ROS 2 Humble, RTAB-Map visual SLAM, and Nav2 on NVIDIA Jetson edge compute bridged through an Arduino Mega low-level controller.

Abstract

Magnetic resonance imaging patient tables routinely exceed three hundred kilograms and require technologists to translate them manually through restrictive corridors—a workflow implicated in both widespread staffing deficits (approximately sixteen-percent MR technologist vacancy) and ergonomic injury cascades exceeding fifty percent among radiologic professionals carrying similar duties. Recent GE prototypes demonstrated autonomy using scanning lidar plus ROS navigating stacks yet explicitly recommended succeeding work retire lidar dominance in favor of dense depth imagery that carries color channels amenable to semantic safeguards.

This CS766 culminating effort operationalizes that directive on Vektor, an MR-inspired phantom cart that isolates software risk from full-scale actuator integration. Across the proposal semester we migrated sensing from four planned Intel RealSense D435i units toward Luxonis OAK-D modules whose Myriad X vision processors execute rectified stereo disparity, neural detection, and IMU ingestion without saturating Jetson GPU resources. On-host ROS 2 nodes fuse depth projections into synthesized laser-style scans feeding RTAB-Map while preparing Nav2 layered costmaps; dual-channel Cytron MDDS30 motor amplification with quadrature decoding remains supervised by an Arduino Mega safety-oriented bridge for PWM, ultrasonic proximity, joystick override, and emergency-stop pathways.

The integrated repository configuration surrounding final recording documents quad-camera surround instrumentation (forward mapping emphasis plus flank and aft depth-derived scans) consonant with the original four-sensor occlusion-mitigation argument, notwithstanding an interim milestone that validated pipelines with two opposing OAK installations. Remaining exposition synthesizes authored LaTeX proposal and midterm narratives with the embodied Vektor codebase now targeting autonomous navigation benchmarks framed in clinic-relevant corridors.

Clinical motivation and research question

Repeated manual shuttling between scan bays heightens throughput bottlenecks and damages door trim when sightlines degrade. Competitive patents and internal GE demonstrations confirm strategic interest while leaving open modality tradeoffs among 2-D laser slices—which cannot discriminate humans from planar clutter—and RGB-D rigs offering spatially registered appearance cues. Visual simultaneous localization meanwhile struggles along featureless drywall yet benefits from inertia-constrained bridging and depth-only geometric alignment where photometric cues falter—all dynamics we inherit as explicit mitigation themes.

The governing research interrogative therefore reads: can depth-first visual SLAM coupled to Nav2 reliably close autonomous traversals inside healthcare-analog corridors while exploiting CNN-derived semantics (person versus static obstruction) to modulate layered cost obedience without lidar scaffolding? Phantom mass keeps iteration cycles agile so production magnet-compatible actuation paths may absorb validated algorithms afterward.

Evolution from proposal to integrated prototype

The February proposal stipulated four corner-mounted Intel RealSense streams, laptop-or-Jetson ambiguity, exhaustive multi-camera calibration, and phased Nav2 rollout concluding in twenty quantitative trials stressing success throughput, shortest-path efficiency ratios, obstacle avoidance integrity, map fidelity, and sub-hundred millisecond sensing-to-command latency envelopes.

Mid-spring experimentation executed a purposeful hardware pivot summarized in our midterm brief: swapping RealSense assemblies for paired OAK-D imagers exploiting on-silicon disparity engines, migrating root filesystem throughput to NVMe-backed Jetson Orin-series storage once SD/eMMC starvation surfaced during synchronized capture, and delegating traversal electronics to Arduino Mega mediated Cytron amplification after an early twelve-volt logic exposure incident mandated amplifier replacement disciplined by hardware e-stop modernization prior to unattended corridor exercise.

The closing integration captured in companion software elevates concurrency to four Luxonis pipelines with dedicated depth-to-scan contributors for lateral and rear vantage points aligning with the phantom cart’s elongated footprint, ultrasonic complementing imminent-field cushioning, joystick supervisory channels, dock alignment experiments where applicable, and continuous RViz supervisory overlays—reflecting matured scope beyond the pairwise feasibility gate while preserving narrative continuity relative to archival LaTeX submissions.

System architecture

Hardware

Computation concentrates on NVIDIA Jetson Orin-class silicon running Ubuntu 22.04. USB3-attached Luxonis DepthAI peripherals stream rectified monochrome pairs, disparity/depth composites, calibrated color topics, BNO085-class inertial trims, while auxiliary nodes synthesize planar range sweeps analogous to wedge lidar scans consumed by RTAB-Map registrations and Nav2 local cost layering. Wheel odometry originates at the Arduino Mega, which multiplexes ultrasonic pair sampling, analog joystick ingestion, supervised zero-velocity relays, PWM directives into the regenerative-capable Cytron MDDS30 channel doubling as thermal headroom against steep grade starts. Mechanical substrates borrow an MRI phantom cart lattice sized near eighty-six × forty-three × eighty-nine centimeters preserving differential kinematics anchored by midpoint wheel pairs and sprung casters simplifying planar unicycle modeling.

Software

ROS 2 launch graphs toggle mapping versus navigation ensembles: camera_publisher fronts central RGB-D ingestion, sibling depth_to_scan amalgam converts peripheral depth envelopes into fused LaserScan composites, base_controller arbitrates joystick versus autonomy velocity requests with serial safeguarding hooks, exploratory scripts exercise spatial detection dualities (dual_detection.py, dual_stereo_depth.py) mirroring midterm methodological detail, and Nav2 activates DWB-compliant trajectory tracking once occupancy grids stabilize after RTAB fusion of depth and IMU-aided closures. DepthAI SDK version three dictates pipeline orchestration—including USB three superspeed enumeration following Myriad bootstrap—and RViz supervisors communicate operator intent alongside optional web mirrors for remote stakeholders.

Perception algorithms and autonomy pipeline

Stereo front-ends rectify epipolar geometry before OAK disparity engines emit dense correspondence fields; extended disparity widens maximal triangulation distance pertinent to tunnel-like clinical reaches while fractional subpixel disparity tightens doorway proximities. Depth hypotheses convert to Cartesian millimeter clouds feeding projection utilities that voxelize inhibitor occupancy for Nav inflation layers awaiting extrinsic chaining once independent calibration solves align opposite mounting axes through shared chessboard solves composited into static tf2 frames.

Myriad-hosted detection graphs evaluated YOLOv6 nano COCO checkpoints alongside SCRFD person specialization so spatial detection nodes annotate three-dimensional centroid hypotheses without exhausting CUDA cycles—critical for ethically weighted human clearance overlays relative to reflective equipment clutter. IMU deltas seed RTAB-Map when visual vocabularies underperform across achromatic corridors, aligning with contingency strategies drafted midterm anticipating depth-only iterative-closest-point supplements if loop closures stagnate.

Estimated latency budgeting from midterm instrumentation remains consistent: aggregated USB ingestion, disparity, asynchronous neural passes, pose graph maintenance, planner cycles, and serial dispatch remain near seventy-five milliseconds on critical paths absent thermal throttling—comfortably beneath the cited one-hundred millisecond supervisory objective.

Mapping, localization, and Nav2 missions

Exploration episodes launch SLAM-tailored parametrizations bridging robot descriptions, calibrated transforms, fused scans, RGB-D registrations, RViz waypoint markers, enabling technologist-style pacing through novel floor plans while logging bag files for regressions offline. Established maps pivot into Nav2 bring-up aligning global planner graphs, smoother velocity outputs, contextual recovery choreography (rotation, backoff), semantics-aware penalties when detection heads assert human occupancy corridors, ultrasonic-triggered freezes, joystick preemption respecting clinical override doctrine, docking alignment scripts when chargers participate, maintaining operational symmetry with ROS community reference stacks yet tuned to elongated cart footprints.

Key engineering challenges encountered

  • Filesystem throughput versus streaming fan-in. Migrating Jetson boots from SD/eMMC congestion to NVMe staging eliminated catastrophic frame starvation during synchronized logging as chronicled midterm documentation.
  • OAK superspeed handshake nuance. Devices initially negotiate USB2 until firmware payloads complete; scripted verification (verify_cameras.py) assures operators reach SuperSpeed before condemning cabling.
  • Power segregation discipline. Accidental bridging of traction pack onto logic rails mandated MDDS30 replacement and galvanized explicit interlock rehearsals ahead of unattended runs.
  • Environmental photometric sparsity. Corridor whiteness dictates IMU-informed odometry bridging, disparity-only supplements, adaptive RTAB feature thresholds enumerated in contingency planning milestones.
  • Safety-critical defaults. Serial watchdog timeouts, microcontroller-enforced braking, and tactile e-stop cabling remain prerequisites before shared hospital adjacency trials escalate.

Evaluation plan

Formal quantitative assessment aligns with proposal commitments—each metric collected across at minimum twenty scripted trials juxtaposing static obstructors, surrogate dynamic actors, longitudinal drift stressors, latency histograms—with qualitative map audits versus annotated floor primitives when architectural drawings exist alongside lidar-derived comparators when permissible.

  • Mission completion rate: Autonomous attainment of commanded pose sans manual rescue or catastrophic stop asserts target ≥80 % throughput under controlled corridors.
  • Path efficiency: Executed spline length divided by plausible shortest bounded clearance quantifies deviation magnitude near unity when DWB behaves nominally.
  • Obstacle rehearsal integrity: Inserted movable barriers and walking confederates quantify costmap fidelity via miss-event accounting.
  • Structural map fidelity: Compare SLAM voxelizations against surveying baselines stressing drift envelopes after elongated loops.
  • Sensing-to-actuation latency: Maintain sub-100 ms bounded worst-case percentile using timestamped tracer spans.
